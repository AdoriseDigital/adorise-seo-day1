3. Value-Based (Outcome-Linked)

Best for: High-confidence ROI, mature relationships, competitive differentiation

Value-Based Formula

Fee = (Client's Expected Annual Value × Your Confidence %) × Your Share %

  • Client expects $500k additional revenue from automation
  • You're 80% confident (based on similar projects)
  • You take 15% of value = $60,000 project fee
  • vs. Time-based: 200 hrs × $200 = $40,000

Result: 50% higher fee, aligned incentives.

Pricing Psychology & Tactics

  • Anchor high: Present three options (Good/Better/Best). Most choose middle.
  • Monthly vs. annual: "Retainer $5,000/mo" vs "$60,000/yr with 2 months free"
  • Scope guardrails: Define "in scope" explicitly. Change orders = new project.
  • Payment terms: 50% upfront, 25% midpoint, 25% delivery. Never net-30 for projects.
  • Maintenance clarity: Separate line item. 15-20% of build cost/month is standard.

Common Pricing Mistakes

Mistake 1: Hourly Billing for Projects

Punishes efficiency. Client pays more when you're slow. Fixed price aligns incentives.

Mistake 2: No Maintenance Pricing

Clients expect free updates forever. Always quote maintenance as separate recurring line.

Mistake 3: Underpricing Discovery

Discovery is high-value consulting. Charge $2,500-5,000 for paid discovery, credit toward project.

Mistake 4: Same Price for All Clients

Enterprise client gets 10x value from same workflow. Price to value, not cost.

FAQ

Should I publish prices on my website?
Publish starting prices ("Packages from $5,000") to filter tire-kickers. Keep custom/enterprise pricing off-site.
How do I handle scope creep?
Change order form: new scope, new timeline, new price. Minimum $1,500 per change order. Build 10% buffer in original quote.
What if client wants value-based but I'm not confident?
Hybrid: time-based floor + value-based upside. "Fee is $30k (time) + 10% of revenue above $300k." Protects you, aligns them.
